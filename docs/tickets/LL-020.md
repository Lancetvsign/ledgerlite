# LL-020 — Chart of Accounts Schema and Validation


**Branch** `feat/ll-020-chart-of-accounts`
**Use plan mode.**

> Implement LL-020: the `accounts` domain model, in one reviewed migration.
>
> Fields: `id` (UUID), `company_id`, `account_number`, `name`, `account_type`,
> `account_subtype`, `parent_account_id` (nullable), `system_account_type` (nullable),
> `description` (nullable), `active`, `created_at`, `updated_at`.
>
> Account types: `ASSET`, `LIABILITY`, `EQUITY`, `REVENUE`, `COGS`, `EXPENSE`.
> Subtypes must be extensible without a migration to the type enum.
>
> **No balance field exists.** Not `balance`, not `current_balance`, not `ytd_total`,
> not a cached anything. Add a comment in the schema file stating that account balances
> are derived from journal lines and that adding a balance column requires an ADR.
> This will be tempting later; make it hard.
>
> **Database-level constraints — required:**
> ```sql
> UNIQUE (company_id, id)                                    -- enables composite FKs
> UNIQUE (company_id, account_number) WHERE account_number IS NOT NULL
> FOREIGN KEY (company_id, parent_account_id)
>   REFERENCES accounts (company_id, id)                     -- parent cannot cross companies
> CHECK (parent_account_id IS NULL OR parent_account_id <> id)
> ```
> The composite parent foreign key is the point: a parent account in another company
> becomes structurally impossible rather than something the service layer remembers to
> check. Verify by attempting the cross-company insert directly in SQL, bypassing the
> application, and confirming the database rejects it.
>
> **Centralized validation rules:** account belongs to the company; parent belongs to
> the same company; name required; valid type; `system_account_type` cannot be
> arbitrarily reassigned; system accounts cannot be deleted; referenced accounts cannot
> be hard-deleted; inactive accounts remain historically available and queryable;
> account numbers unique within a company when present. Prevent hard deletion through
> application services entirely — per ADR-006, accounting records are deactivated, never
> destroyed.
>
> Guard against cycles in the parent hierarchy — A → B → A must be rejected.
>
> Every query is company-scoped and goes through the LL-013 authorization layer.
>
> **Tests:** one per validation rule, plus cross-company parent rejection at both the
> service layer and the raw SQL layer, plus hierarchy cycle rejection.
> Register `accounts` in the LL-014 isolation harness.
> Document protected account behavior in `/docs/ACCOUNTING_RULES.md`.
